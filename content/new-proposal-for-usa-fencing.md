There are significant issues with the current allocation of tournaments by geography by USA Fencing.  The current bid system reinforces this inequity and I would suggest should be scrapped. 

What I would suggest is to create an area designation of zones that represent 2-3 different types of markets. 

1. Active - a contiguous geographic area with at least xxx active competitive fencers
2. Developing - contiguous areas with less than xxx fencers but large populations
3. Diffuse - areas with fencing schools but less than xxx fencers but diffuse populations 
4. Zero

These zones can be sub parts of regions or separate.  

Active Zones should be entitled to a set of tournaments that support a full path promotion. Meaning A-ranking tournaments, sufficient tournaments to qualify for JOs, Nationals, etc. 

Developing Zones are ones where there is significant opportunity to grow the sport. There, youth tournaments should be prioritized. 

Diffuse Zones are the hardest to support, but a good opportunity for local schools to host sanctioned tournaments. 

Once those criteria are established then the bidding process starts.  

Using examples from USA Swimming and other sports. Bids include a full P&L. Fees are uncapped to reflect for different COLA but lower proposed fees are given points. New hosts can earn their way up a ladder to larger events by hosting successful previous events.
